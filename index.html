What Is Happening to Music Criticism?
Ted Gioia · The Honest Broker · 2024

Gioia tracked the collapse of the full-time critic beat, city by city: the staff positions cut, the sections folded, the expertise that once mediated between listeners and an overwhelming catalog quietly defunded. His point isn't nostalgia for the byline. It's that when nobody's paid to listen closely and say so in public, the vacuum gets filled by whoever has the cheapest way to generate opinions at scale.

Flashlight reads the move as the moment the disclosure mark started setting the price. Whatever Substack's detector is today, TIDAL is the demonstration that a label rarely stays descriptive once money is attached.

The Blues: LOCKSS it up!

The last word goes to the archive. A Different Lens splits AI blues fraud into two kinds: the fantasy fake, which announces its own fiction, and the archive fake, which impersonates a real 1962 John Lee Hooker release with no credits and no history to check. The second kind does not need to fool a listener; it needs to fool the algorithm, and the person who finds it a decade from now. Labels, stickers, and scanners are all guarding the same thing, the record of who actually made what.
