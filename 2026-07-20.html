Kendrick Lamar: The Rolling Stone Interview
Rolling Stone · Rolling Stone · 2015

Caught mid-ascent between *good kid, m.A.A.d city* and *To Pimp a Butterfly*, Lamar walks through his obsessive process: the survivor's guilt, the Compton loyalties, the refusal to release a verse until it earns its place. A useful reminder that the most influential voices are usually the most deliberate ones.

First World Cup Halftime Show: Lots of Stars, Little Purpose

Times pop critic Lindsay Zoladz counts the cast for the World Cup's first-ever halftime show (Madonna, BTS, Bieber, the Muppets doing "Seven Nation Army") and finds the problem isn't talent but purpose. I asked AI about myself and was horrified.

AI as Normal Technology

What will be left for us to work on?

AI as Normal Technology pushes back on the superintelligence timeline by pointing to something the race-to-AGI crowd tends to skip: a surprising number of tasks have inherent limits, and we're already near them. The weather prediction analogy is useful. Human performance, the argument goes, is constrained by learning and tools, not biology, which means the ceiling is higher than it looks and harder to automate away than anyone is admitting.
