An Economic Argument for Piracy
Damon Krukowski · Dada Drummer · 2024

Krukowski runs the numbers most artists avoid: when streaming pays fractions of a cent per play, unauthorized sharing isn't theft against the musician so much as a rational response to a payment structure that already extracts most of the value. The provocation lands because it inverts the usual moral framing. Piracy becomes a symptom of platform economics, not a threat to them, and the artist was never the one getting paid anyway.

A Modest Proposal

Dada Drummer Almanach, Damon Krukowski's long-running dispatch on the economics of being a working musician, lands on a fix that's almost insultingly simple: pay the artists people actually listen to. Where do I begin with... Daniel Johnston?

Here's a career that resists the usual climb-to-greatness narrative: The Line of Best Fit argues Daniel Johnston peaked at the start, with 1983's Hi, How Are You (yes, the Cobain T-shirt album). His childlike, cassette-recorder intimacy was inseparable from his fragility, so producers and labels could only distort it. The pitch: start at the beginning, because fame diluted the magic.

Musical Urbanism

The Gun Club live on record, pt. 2: Death party around the world (1983-84)

Leonard Nevarez makes the case that a scrappy 1983 bootleg can outshine polished releases when the band's on fire. His deep-dive on the Gun Club's Italy and Australia dates argues that Kid Congo Powers' return, plus audience chatter bleeding through a floor-recorded mix, captures a road show finding its groove mid-chaos. A collector's argument for imperfection as intimacy.
