Something Has Changed for AI Music on Spotify
Zinstrel has been tracking AI-generated music's quiet colonization of Spotify's algorithmic playlists for months, and this piece catches something shifting in the data: the platform appears to be adjusting how AI tracks surface, though whether that's policy, pressure, or just algorithmic drift remains unclear. The Andre Gee Newsletter · Music & Scene
Author Lambros Fatsis On UK Drill Rap (Basically) Being Illegal
The Andre Gee Newsletter interviews Lambros Fatsis about UK drill's legal persecution — not metaphorical persecution, but actual courtroom consequences where lyrics become evidence and creativity becomes criminalized. Fatsis has written the book on this (literally), and the conversation cuts through the hand-wringing about "violent music" to examine how the British legal system has constructed a framework where a genre is effectively prosecutable. If you've followed the moral panics around drill but haven't tracked how those panics translate into actual sentencing, this fills in the gap.

02 Music & Scene Music
JazzWax
When Jazz Became High Culture
Marc Myers tracks jazz's slow conversion from underworld music to certified high culture — Carnegie Hall as early as 1912, the long Prohibition detour through speakeasies and rum-runners, big bands adapting the music for dance-hall syncopation in the '30s, and finally a 1957 plateau on par with classical that, by Myers's count, only held for about eight years. The argument cuts against the assumption that records alone conferred legitimacy: phonographs, rock'n'roll, and television each had a hand in pushing jazz across the line.

03 Music Industry Analysis Music
STVDIO
Some artists are converting thousands of fans to their owned audience. But how exactly?
musicben revisits the perennial 'own your audience' refrain with actual numbers — 200 million data points from Laylo, the fan-data tool behind Sabrina Carpenter and Skrillex campaigns — and pulls out three concrete conversion windows: pre-release hype that creates sign-up fomo, passive sign-ups via website and socials (Instagram converts best), and the tour cycle as the highest-leverage moment of all. The piece's value is in moving the conversation past 'you should do this' to 'this is when, and roughly how often.'
Your note
Bruce Ramos
PE Bought Hipgnosis at $2.5 Billion. They're Selling It for $4 Billion in 24 Months. Here's the Operator Math.
The Blackstone-Hipgnosis flip wasn't a music bet — it was a private equity playbook that ran on top of music for 24 months. Bruce Ramos breaks down the operator logic behind the $1.5B spread: Blackstone priced the gap between public-market sentiment and private-asset reality, structured a clean exit, and moved fast. The piece's real argument is that the same gap exists at the indie tier, in Latin catalogs, in Regional Mexican — anywhere sellers haven't matched buyer sophistication. The buyer side has gotten institutional. The sell side hasn't caught up.

Music Business Worldwide
Want to talk about 'superfans'? Ask Live Nation.
While the recorded music side has spent two years theorizing about superfan monetization — platform deals, D2C startups, Goldman Sachs TAM estimates — Live Nation is just building bigger VIP rooms. Music Business Worldwide covers CEO Michael Rapino's Q1 2026 vision for remaking concert venues along sports-arena lines: suites, private boxes, premium entrances, dedicated F&B. The argument implicit in Rapino's framing is that live has always understood superfans better than streaming ever will, because it already charges them accordingly.

Bloomberg reports Sony Music is in exclusive talks to acquire Blackstone's Recognition Music Group — home to catalogs from Justin Bieber, Neil Young, and others — in a deal valued at $3.5 to $4 billion. Music Business Worldwide
Udio Admits to Scraping YouTube Audio for AI Training in Answer to Sony Music Lawsuit
Music Business Worldwide reports that Udio, facing a copyright infringement suit from Sony Music and its Arista labels, has confirmed what the industry long suspected: its models were trained on audio scraped from YouTube. Udio's framing — that the system derived "statistical insights about auditory characteristics" rather than copying recordings — is the standard fair-use repositioning every AI music company is testing right now. How courts receive that argument will set the baseline for the whole sector.

The Nelson George Mixtape
Nostalgia for Jellybeans
The Nelson George Mixtape uses the jellybean — Reagan's favorite candy, and the object placed before Black voters asked to count them as a condition of suffrage — as the through-line for an essay on nostalgia as political drug. The piece responds to the Supreme Court's 2026 Louisiana v. Callais ruling, which used "colorblind" logic to gut voting protections in place since 1965. The argument: MAGA nostalgia isn't innocent sentimentality. It's a craving for the specific comfort of a rigged system.
Your note
After School
Subliminalmaxxing and Lo-Fi Rebellion
After School maps a genuinely new cultural positioning: "subliminals" — ambient or ASMR videos layering affirmations at frequencies designed to bypass conscious rejection — reframed as the female equivalent of male looksmaxxing culture. The format has existed since at least 2012, but the wellness-meets-body-modification framing is fresh. Creators claim theta frequencies and overnight looping produce "instant results," and the audience is real — one profiled creator has 150K subscribers. For anyone tracking how ambient audio genres acquire behavioral and identity functions, this is worth watching.
