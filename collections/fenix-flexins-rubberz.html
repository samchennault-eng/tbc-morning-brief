STORYLINE #1

Me and the Devil: Fenix Flexin's AI Authorship Fight

9 articles · Tracking since Jul 2026

One of the summer’s hottest songs is also one of the most controversial. “Rubberz,” the left-field hit from LA rapper Fenix Flexin, delivers street-rap verses in a put-on British accent over '80s synth-pop. It’s an unexpected, weirdly compelling earworm of a song, and also maybe not real? Since it dropped, audiences, critics, and town criers on both sides of the music’s AI discussion have been debating whether this is purely Flexin’s concoction or he used AI to create his hit.

For this Storyline, we’ll trace the story from the early rumblings and denials to the evidence drop that strongly suggested that Flexin was juicin’, and we’ll also capture the postscript commentary and root this in the historical precedent of other music industry debates around authorship and ownership. And we’ll present this through contemporaneous coverage from some of music’s finest minds, linked out and annotated below. With its twists and turns, and its rich layer of news and commentary, it’s just the type of story the Storyline format was designed to capture.

There’s no chart policy, no shared detection standard, nor any disclosure rules, and music's first doping controversy is being run without a referee. And we’ve been here before. Sampling's definitional crisis ended in a fee schedule; lip-sync ended in Milli Vanilli's revoked Grammy; and ghostwriting settled into a quiet credit economy. Each break was resolved with a payout instead of a definition. It’s too early to know who’ll be cutting the check for this one, but we’re all tuned in.

Zinstrel - AI Music Culture & Analysis Marcus Lawrence Jun 26, 2026

Fenix Flexin: The AI Music Hit Hiding on the Hot 100

HumanStandard cleared "Rubberz" as human-made twice before it got it right. Marcus Lawrence's Zinstrel traces why. Treblo, a free generator that stacks separate vocal and instrumental models, sits outside the training data most detectors rely on. A community tip sent founder Rasha Rahman to scrape a thousand Treblo tracks and retrain, and the song came back fully AI-generated. Asked point-blank whether "Rubberz" was made on his platform, Treblo's founder declined to say. Transparent AI creators get mocked, Lawrence adds, while a denial with major distribution behind it keeps climbing. What now?!?

How Do You Define An 'AI Song?'

Fenix's own defense creates the problem Andre Gee circles. Purps merely put autotune and reverb on his vocals, Fenix insisted to two men on an LA street, half-heartedly reciting his Morrissey-channeling bars as proof. Take him at his word and the song is studio processing of a human performance; the doubters hear AI vocals. That is the new gray area Gee names, and every answer indicts somebody. The album snippet Fenix played on Channel 5, he notes, sounds much more like his regular voice.

My Number One Song Is Not AI

Sam Hockley-Smith is tired of wondering whether art was made by a person, and "Rubberz" made him do it anyway. Historical Precedents
POW MAG Doc Zeus Aug 3, 2015

The Spotlight Makes You Nervous: Why It Matters That Drake Isn't Writing His Own Songs

"Authorship is everything," Doc Zeus wrote at POW MAG in 2015, watching Drake beat Meek Mill in public while Quentin Miller's leaked reference tracks corroborated the accusation underneath. The essay's real subject is the price of the question: confessional music trades on the presumption its author lived it, so proof of another pen bleeds value from every song. Drake won the beef and kept the charts. What he lost, Doc Zeus argues, is anyone ever fully believing he writes his raps again.

NPR Music Oliver Wang May 1, 2013

20 Years Ago Biz Markie Got The Last Laugh

Music has been here before. In 1991 Gilbert O'Sullivan sued Biz Markie over the piano loop in "Alone Again," and Judge Kevin Duffy's ruling, with $250,000 in damages and a referral to criminal court, ended sampling's anything-goes era. Oliver Wang's NPR retrospective follows what the industry built instead of a definition: clearance departments, buried and obscured lifts, a shadow catalog of songs unreleasable because the paperwork failed. Biz answered two years later with an album titled All Samples Cleared!, the samples itemized on the back cover.

Where It Stands
Stems Nathan Brackett Jul 3, 2026

The Hot 100's First AI Doping Controversy is Here

Music is running its first doping controversy, Stems' Nathan Brackett writes, and like cycling's, it may never resolve. No shared detection standard exists across streamers, distributors, labels, or publishers, so the verdict on "Rubberz" stays he-said-she-said while the song sits in the Hot 100's 60s. The Stems team typed Fenix's name into Treblo themselves and got vocals uncannily close to the record. Fenix, meanwhile, says the all-'80s album is "basically done." In the end, there's no ending to this story, which is the story.
