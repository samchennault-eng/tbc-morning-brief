The AI-inflected crisis artists are facing, in 4 charts

The Gig · Music & Scene
Irrational Number: Pi Recordings at 25
Nate Chinen's profile of Pi Recordings at 25 is ostensibly a label retrospective, but it's really a case study in how to build a catalog with curatorial integrity intact. The label's bet — that adventurous jazz and improvised music could sustain a coherent identity across decades — looks almost quaint against today's playlist-first logic. Chinen, one of the sharpest jazz writers working, traces the decisions that kept Pi legible as a voice rather than just a release schedule. For anyone thinking about what editorial coherence means at catalog scale, this is the model.
Label strategy
Bruce Ramos · Music Industry Analysis
Corridos Is 92.8% Indie. Indies Earn 2x Per Stream of the Superstars.
Bruce Ramos digs into the corridos streaming economy and surfaces a counterintuitive finding: indie artists in the genre earn roughly double per stream what the superstars do. The math traces back to how catalog depth, listener behavior, and label deal structures interact differently at different scales. It's a useful corrective to the assumption that streaming economics punish independents uniformly — the reality is more textured, and Ramos walks through the mechanics clearly.

02 Music & Scene Music
2020 Music Group
Flat Tire, Ear Procedure, Millennial Text-Sound
2020 Music Group traces a specific window — roughly 2017 to 2022 — when experimental and computer musicians turned heavily toward text-sound composition, reviving Fluxus and post-Cagean word-score traditions through digital tools like text-to-speech, vocoders, and DAW-native sampling. The argument that makes this worth reading: that moment was also peak flat-meme culture, and the two weren't incidental — they shared a logic of image, text, and loose representation. That framing has since migrated into short-form video, and the piece is honest that something got lost in the translation.

Cuba on Record
Cristóbal Díaz Ayala, A Patron Saint of Cuban Music, Has Died
Cuba on Record remembers Cristóbal Díaz Ayala, the Cuban-born scholar whose obsessive, card-catalogued devotion produced the largest archive of Cuban music in the world — now housed at FIU. The collection spans a 1900 soprano recording to the first documented Afro-Cuban jazz fusion cut to Cachao's earliest recorded bass solo. It's the kind of obit that doubles as an argument for what archival work actually does: it makes streaming catalogs possible in the first place.

Brad Mehldau Substack
Jon Caramanica is a bad cliché
A working musician's case against the archetypal pop critic who mistakes cultural fluency for musical understanding. The Brad Mehldau Substack uses the New York Times' exclusion of Billy Joel from its Greatest Songwriters list as the launching point for a sharper argument: critics like Jon Caramanica treat pop as a sociological event rather than a musical one, and their ignorance of craft produces confident wrongness. The Billy Joel debate is almost beside the point — this is really about what criticism loses when it can't hear what's actually happening in the music.

03 Music Industry Analysis Music
STVDIO
Some artists are converting thousands of fans to their owned audience. But how exactly?
musicben's piece in STVDIO tackles the gap between knowing you should own your audience and actually knowing how to do it. Drawing on 200 million data points from fan-conversion platform Laylo — which runs campaigns for Sabrina Carpenter and Skrillex — the piece maps three high-conversion moments: pre-release teases, passive sign-up flows (Instagram leads), and tour cycles. The Skrillex Dropbox album drop is the marquee case study, but the piece tries to show how the same mechanics scale down for independent artists.

Hip-Hop Media And The Violence Of "Power"
The andre gee newsletter takes aim at Complex's most-powerful-in-hip-hop-media list and the organizing logic behind it — namely, that "power" means sparking viral rap debates. 04 AI + Content & Editorial Technology AI
Contraptions
Ribbonfarm Resurrected
Contraptions documents the build-out of a RAG-based AI agent trained on a writer's own archive — and the more interesting finding is buried in the methodology: fine-tuning (teaching a model to write like you) lost out to retrieval (teaching a model to pull from what you actually wrote). The distinction matters. One produces mimicry, the other produces something closer to a searchable intellectual record. The infrastructure stack — Voyage.ai embeddings, Pinecone vectors, Claude API for tagging — is worth noting for anyone thinking about what corpus-based editorial tools actually require.
Your note
(Re)Structured News | Tow-Knight Center at CUNY
Digestion
The Tow-Knight Center's (Re)Structured News makes a useful conceptual move here: the attention economy is giving way to an intention economy. When content production costs collapse and volume becomes infinite, the valuable thing isn't a feed — it's a filter built around what you've decided matters. The piece walks through a DIY AI news monitor as proof of concept, but the real argument is that designing your own signal-from-noise system is itself an act of editorial judgment.

Look What You Made Me Do
I'm Going to Punch Your Face: A Short History of Keeping Parties Polite!
Eugene S. Robinson's piece uses a 1982 confrontation with Glenn Danzig at San Francisco's Elite Club as the entry point for something older and more structural: the question of who enforces basic decency when institutions won't. The inciting incident — Danzig's band dropping a homophobic slur at a queer audience, followed by a guitarist swinging his instrument into the back of a kid's skull — is vivid and specific. Robinson's frame is that social contracts in live music spaces have always been maintained, informally and physically, from the floor up.

As any musician knows, the work and the moment have to collide. Here they did.
